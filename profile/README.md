# AI Usage at CatCodeLabs
We believe good software comes from people who understand, wrote, and care about the code they ship. We write the absolute majority of our code ourselves, as we find that process more engaging, more reliable for long-term maintenance, and, ultimately, more fun (which, honestly, is what all this is about).
> We do use AI tools in limited, targeted ways as assistants, but **never** as the primary authors of our projects. We are not vibe coders, and we are determined to never be classified as such.
## Where we do use AI
- Debugging and investigation on our projects. When we hit difficult issues, we sometimes consult AI models for explanations, hypotheses, or candidate fixes. **We treat the output exclusively as input to our own analysis and fixes, never as the final statement.**
- Early exploration and initial UI/UX design sketches on certain pre-release projects. AI can help generate starting points or layout ideas that can give us a place to start, or act as placeholders until we finish the final UI design. A scaffolding, one could say. <sub>Note: "pre-release" refers to any version before 1.0</sub>
## Where we do not rely on AI
- We **do not** generate the bulk of production or client-facing code via AI and accept it with minimal review.
- We **do not** treat AI output as a substitute for understanding architecture, ownership rules (especially important in our current main language, Rust), edge cases, security, or long-term maintainability.
- Core design decisions, final implementations, testing strategy, and accountability remain with the **human** developers on the team.
## Our standards
Every **suggestion** we take from an AI tool is reviewed, understood, tested, and adapted (or dismissed) by a person who can explain what the resulting code does and why it belongs (or doesn't belong) in the project. If we cannot stand behind a change, it simply will not be shipped. We prefer slower, deliberate work that builds real skill and durable systems over maximum speed via heavy automation.
At the core of everything we do sits the enjoyment of actually writing software. Tools will continue to evolve, and we will re-evaluate our practices as they do. What will not change is our commitment to shipping code that humans understand and are responsible for. Did we accidentally brick your device? The finger points to a human, not an AI.
If you have questions about how we work or how AI factors into a specific project, feel free to ask us on Discord, or write us an E-Mail if you're feeling old-fashioned (or simply don't have Discord).
